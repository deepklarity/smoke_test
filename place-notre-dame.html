Notre Dame Cathedral

A masterpiece of French Gothic architecture on the Île de la Cité — a symbol of resilience, faith, and centuries of Parisian history.

Back to all places

A Gothic Masterpiece Born in Fire

Construction of Notre Dame de Paris began in 1163 under Bishop Maurice de Sully and continued for nearly 200 years, finally completing around 1345. The cathedral pioneered many architectural innovations that defined the French Gothic style: the use of flying buttresses to support taller walls and larger windows, the ribbed vault to distribute weight more efficiently, and the emphasis on verticality and light that came to define cathedral design across Europe. At its completion, Notre Dame was the largest church in the Western world, a title it held for centuries.

The cathedral's facade showcases the evolution of Gothic art across its three portals: the Portal of the Last Judgment (center), the Portal of the Virgin (north), and the Portal of Saint Anne (south). Above them, the famous rose windows — particularly the south rose at 12.9 metres in diameter — rank among the largest in the world. The north tower houses the great bourdon bell Emmanuel, cast in 1681 and famously tolled throughout French history, from royal coronations to the liberation of Paris in 1944.

What to Experience Today

Following the devastating fire of April 2019, Notre Dame is undergoing an ambitious restoration to rebuild the spire, roof, and damaged vaulting. While the interior remains closed for restoration, visitors can still experience the cathedral's exterior and the archaeological crypt beneath the parvis, which displays remains from the Roman and medieval eras uncovered during excavation work. The adjacent Saint-Jean square hosts regular events and performances that keep the site's cultural legacy alive.

The crown of spire fragments, displayed at the fire's first anniversary, serves as a poignant reminder of the structure's former glory. Plans call for the spire to be rebuilt identically to Viollet-le-Duc's 19th-century design, using traditional materials and techniques. The restoration, one of the most complex in history, is expected to complete by the end of 2024, with the cathedral reopening to the public in late 2024 or early 2025 — in time for the 2024 Summer Olympics and the 2025 millennium anniversary of the cathedral's founding.

Visitor Tips

Check the official restoration schedule before visiting, as access to the cathedral grounds may be limited during construction. The best views of the cathedral are from across the Seine on the Left Bank or from the Pont de la Cité, which offers a dramatic frontal perspective. For a different angle, cross to the Right Bank and photograph the apse from the Square Viviani, where you'll also find one of the oldest trees in Paris. Once reopened, book timed-entry tickets online — free entry is available to all visitors, but reserved slots help manage the expected crowds.
