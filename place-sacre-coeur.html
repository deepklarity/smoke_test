Sacré-Cœur Basilica

A luminous white sanctuary atop Montmartre hill — offering spiritual solace, sweeping Parisian panoramas, and one of the world's largest mosaics.

Back to all places

A Promise Kept

The Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Paris stands as a monumental fulfilment of a solemn vow made in 1870, during the Franco-Prussian War. As Paris lay under siege and faced the humiliation of defeat, two prominent businessmen pledged to build a church dedicated to the Sacred Heart of Jesus if France was spared further catastrophe. After decades of political turbulence, including the Paris Commune, construction finally began in 1875 and was completed in 1914 — though the basilica was not consecrated until after World War I in 1919.

Built in a striking Romano-Byzantine style that deliberately contrasts with the Gothic Notre Dame, Sacré-Cœur's brilliant white travertine stone contains calcite, which naturally exudes calcite when it rains, keeping the basilica remarkably white even after more than a century. The building's five distinctive domes dominate the Montmartre skyline, visible from across Paris and serving as a beacon to both the faithful and the merely curious.

What to See & Experience

Step inside to encounter the apse's awe-inspiring mosaic — one of the largest in the world — depicting the Sacred Heart in glory, surrounded by figures from French history including Joan of Arc and King Louis IX. The vast interior, with its glowing gold mosaics and ornate decoration, offers a striking contrast to the stark, austere exterior. The crypt, though often overlooked, contains impressive architectural features and a quiet space for reflection.

For many visitors, the highlight is the climb to the dome. The 300-step spiral staircase leads to one of Paris's most spectacular viewpoints, offering 360-degree panoramas that stretch to the distant skyscrapers of La Défense. Unlike the Eiffel Tower, this vantage point includes the Eiffel Tower itself in the composition — a unique photographic opportunity. The esplanade in front of the basilica, always filled with street performers, artists, and visitors, provides a sweeping view of Paris without the climb.

Visitor Tips

Visit early in the morning or around sunset to avoid the crowds and capture the basilica bathed in golden light. The climb to the dome is separate from the basilica entry and costs a small fee — but the unparalleled views are worth every euro. Photography inside the basilica is permitted but be respectful of worshippers; the atmosphere is markedly more spiritual and solemn than many other tourist sites. After your visit, explore the winding streets of Montmartre — Place du Tertre with its resident artists, the historic Lapin Agile cabaret, and the café where Picasso and Dalí once debated art are all within walking distance.
